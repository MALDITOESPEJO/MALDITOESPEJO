# MALDITOESPEJO — DISCOVERY VS PUBLICATION SOURCE POLICY

## 1. Purpose

This policy separates the sources used to **find a story** from the sources used to **support what MALDITOESPEJO publishes**.

MALDITOESPEJO may use any useful source as an editorial radar. That does not make the source evidence for publication.

## 2. Core distinction

### Discovery source

A `DISCOVERY_SOURCE` is used to:

- detect that a relevant development may have occurred;
- locate a document, dataset, statement, image or event;
- identify a claim that deserves investigation;
- discover a lead or a possible story angle.

A discovery source is an **internal research input**. It does not automatically appear in the published article.

### Publication source

A `PUBLICATION_SOURCE` is a source whose material has been reviewed and is actually used to support, establish or properly attribute information published by MALDITOESPEJO.

The publication source should normally be the strongest available source for the specific fact: official record, original dataset, court or regulator record, direct observation, original document, qualified research or other appropriate primary/independent evidence.

## 3. Public article rule

A discovery source must not be mentioned in the published article merely because it led the newsroom to the story.

Do not publish formulations such as:

- "Según [otro medio]..." when that medium was only used to discover the story;
- "[Otro medio] informó primero..." unless this is itself materially relevant to the story;
- links to the discovery article as if it were supporting evidence;
- a visible "source of detection" field;
- a bibliography entry for a medium that supplied only the initial lead.

The public article should cite the evidence that supports the published proposition.

## 4. Internal traceability

The discovery source remains part of the internal case history when recording it is useful for auditability.

Canonical internal chain:

`DISCOVERY_SOURCE → LEAD → INVESTIGATION → PRIMARY / APPROPRIATE EVIDENCE → VERIFICATION → PUBLICATION`

This preserves the history of how the newsroom found the story without confusing discovery with evidence.

## 5. A source may have both roles

A source can be both discovery and publication source, but the roles must be recorded separately.

Example:

- a government release alerts the newsroom to a new measure;
- the same official release contains the exact figures published in the article.

In that case it can be recorded as both `DISCOVERY_SOURCE` and `PUBLICATION_SOURCE` for the relevant propositions.

## 6. Secondary media

News agencies, newspapers, broadcasters, specialist media and other secondary sources may be essential for discovery, chronology, context and cross-checking.

They do not replace primary evidence when primary evidence is reasonably obtainable and necessary for the proposition being published.

Repeated secondary reports sharing the same underlying dispatch are not independent corroboration.

## 7. Public-source selection

For every material published assertion, the newsroom should be able to answer:

1. What source supports it?
2. What exact evidence was examined?
3. Is the source primary, independent or secondary?
4. Does the evidence establish the proposition or merely report somebody else's claim?
5. Has the source been revised or corrected?
6. Is the attribution visible where necessary?

## 8. Language rule

The source architecture must not force readers to understand newsroom terminology.

Internal labels such as `DISCOVERY_SOURCE`, `PUBLICATION_SOURCE`, `LINEAGE`, `EVIDENCE`, `CLAIM` and `VERIFICATION` belong to the editorial system.

Published journalism should use plain, precise language that a general reader can understand.

Examples:

- Internal: `VERIFIED_PRIMARY_DATA`
- Public: "Los datos oficiales muestran..."

- Internal: `ATTRIBUTED_CLAIM`
- Public: "El ministro afirmó..."

- Internal: `UNRESOLVED_PROPOSITION`
- Public: "Todavía no hay datos que permitan determinar..."

## 9. Prohibited shortcut

Never use the existence of a published article by another outlet as a substitute for doing the underlying verification when the original evidence is obtainable.

Core rule:

> **Usamos los medios para encontrar historias; usamos la evidencia para contar las historias.**

## 10. Editorial doctrine

MALDITOESPEJO does not reproduce another medium's story as its own. It uses the available information to identify what deserves investigation, reconstructs the relevant facts from the best available evidence, verifies them, attributes statements correctly and writes the result independently.
