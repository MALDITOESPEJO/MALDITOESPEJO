# MALDITOESPEJO — POST-PUBLICATION INTEGRITY POLICY

## 1. Principle

Publication is not the end of verification.

Every consequential article remains subject to post-publication monitoring because sources revise data, courts issue new decisions, conflicts evolve and previously unavailable evidence may change the assessment.

## 2. Monitoring triggers

A published item should be reconsidered when:

- a primary source issues a correction or revision;
- a material new fact changes the event state;
- credible evidence contradicts a central claim;
- a source is found to have manipulated or misrepresented evidence;
- a dataset is revised materially;
- a legal or regulatory decision changes the relevant status;
- a verification failure is discovered;
- a significant attribution error is identified.

## 3. Response hierarchy

`NO_ACTION → NOTE/CLARIFICATION → UPDATE → CORRECT → RETRACT`

The response should be proportional to the significance of the problem.

## 4. Preservation

The system should preserve:

- original publication state;
- publication timestamp;
- subsequent versions;
- correction/update timestamp;
- reason for change;
- evidence that triggered the change;
- editor responsible for the decision.

## 5. No silent history rewriting

Material factual changes must not be made invisibly.

The objective is not to expose internal tooling to readers unnecessarily, but to maintain an auditable institutional record.

## 6. Post-publication confidence

A publication can move between states:

`VERIFIED → CHALLENGED → PARTIALLY_VERIFIED → CORRECTED`

or, where warranted:

`VERIFIED → CONTRADICTED → RETRACTED`

These are states of the editorial record, not claims about absolute reality.

## 7. Legal and reputational sensitivity

For allegations involving identifiable persons or organizations, post-publication review should apply heightened scrutiny to attribution, evidence quality, procedural status and language of certainty.

## 8. Closure

A corrected or retracted item remains part of the audit history. Its previous existence must not be erased from the internal provenance chain.
