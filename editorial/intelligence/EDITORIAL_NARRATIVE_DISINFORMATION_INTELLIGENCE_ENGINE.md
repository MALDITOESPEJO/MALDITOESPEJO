# EDITORIAL NARRATIVE & DISINFORMATION INTELLIGENCE ENGINE

## 1. Purpose

This engine provides structured analysis of narratives, disputed claims, misinformation and potential information operations while preserving strict distinctions between factual error, misleading context, propaganda, coordinated activity and deliberate disinformation.

It is an intelligence and verification layer, not an automated accusation system.

## 2. Canonical model

`OBSERVATION → CLAIM → NARRATIVE → PROPAGATION → EVIDENCE → VERIFICATION → ASSESSMENT`

Where evidence indicates coordinated activity, a separate:

`ACTOR/NETWORK → ACTIVITY → OBJECTIVE/HYPOTHESIS`

may be assessed, but only with independent evidence.

## 3. Fundamental distinctions

- `EVENT ≠ CLAIM`
- `CLAIM ≠ NARRATIVE`
- `NARRATIVE ≠ DISINFORMATION`
- `ERROR ≠ DECEPTION`
- `PROPAGATION ≠ COORDINATION`
- `COORDINATION ≠ MALICIOUS INTENT`
- `VIRALITY ≠ IMPORTANCE`
- `FALSE ≠ DISINFORMATION`

These distinctions are mandatory.

## 4. Claim decomposition

Every material narrative assessment begins with individual claims:

1. exact proposition;
2. claimant or origin;
3. publication/channel;
4. time;
5. jurisdiction/geography;
6. evidence supplied;
7. independent evidence;
8. contradiction;
9. current verification status;
10. material consequence.

Composite narratives must be decomposed before assessment.

## 5. Narrative object

A narrative is a recurring interpretive frame or connected set of claims that gives meaning to events.

A narrative record should contain:

- narrative identifier;
- constituent claims;
- origin/earliest known appearance;
- major propagation nodes;
- supporting evidence;
- contradictory evidence;
- affected jurisdictions/topics;
- temporal evolution;
- attribution;
- uncertainty;
- human assessment.

## 6. Information disorder taxonomy

Use separate classifications:

- `ACCURATE`
- `INACCURATE`
- `MISLEADING`
- `OUT_OF_CONTEXT`
- `UNVERIFIED`
- `DISPUTED`
- `MANIPULATED`
- `SATIRE/PARODY`
- `IMPOSTOR_CONTENT`
- `FABRICATED_CONTENT`
- `FALSE_ATTRIBUTION`
- `UNCERTAIN`

A separate intent field may record:

`UNKNOWN`, `ACCIDENTAL`, `UNDETERMINED`, `SUSPECTED_DELIBERATE`, `ESTABLISHED_DELIBERATE`.

Intent requires evidence and should not be inferred from falsity alone.

## 7. Narrative propagation

Use the information-flow layer to distinguish:

`ORIGIN → AMPLIFICATION → TRANSFORMATION → CROSS-PLATFORM PROPAGATION → CORRECTION/COUNTER-NARRATIVE`

Repeated copies of one upstream claim remain one evidentiary lineage unless independent evidence appears.

## 8. Coordination indicators

Potential indicators may include:

- synchronized publication timing;
- repeated uncommon wording;
- shared assets;
- coordinated hashtag/topic bursts;
- common infrastructure;
- reciprocal amplification;
- common source material;
- repeated identical factual anomalies.

These are indicators, not proof of coordination.

## 9. Coordination evidence standard

A coordination assessment should identify:

- observed behavior;
- independently verifiable data;
- alternative explanations;
- temporal relationship;
- network relationship;
- confidence;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- reviewer.

Do not infer coordinated behavior merely because accounts share a claim.

## 10. Disinformation assessment

`DISINFORMATION` should only be used where the evidence supports both a materially false/misleading information component and sufficient evidence of deliberate deceptive dissemination under the applicable editorial standard.

Where intent cannot be established, prefer an evidence-based description such as `FALSE`, `MISLEADING`, `OUT_OF_CONTEXT`, `UNVERIFIED` or `COORDINATION_SUSPECTED`.

## 11. Counter-evidence

The engine must actively seek disconfirming evidence rather than only evidence supporting an initial hypothesis.

Required checks:

- strongest contrary source;
- primary evidence;
- independent lineage;
- temporal contradiction;
- geographic contradiction;
- methodological challenge;
- plausible alternative explanation.

## 12. Narrative drift

Track changes in:

- certainty;
- attribution;
- scope;
- causality;
- chronology;
- geography;
- numbers;
- emotional framing;
- omission of qualifications.

A narrative may become materially different without any underlying event changing.

## 13. Corrections and counter-narratives

Corrections, debunks and counter-narratives must be linked to the specific claims they address.

A correction does not automatically establish that every related narrative is false.

## 14. Impact assessment

Narrative priority may consider:

- public-safety impact;
- electoral impact;
- health impact;
- conflict/security impact;
- financial/economic impact;
- legal/institutional impact;
- reach and propagation;
- vulnerability of affected populations;
- likelihood of consequential harm.

Reach alone does not establish importance or falsity.

## 15. Alert classes

- `NARRATIVE_EMERGING`
- `NARRATIVE_RAPID_PROPAGATION`
- `NARRATIVE_FALSE_CORROBORATION`
- `NARRATIVE_CLAIM_DRIFT`
- `NARRATIVE_ATTRIBUTION_LOSS`
- `NARRATIVE_MANIPULATED_MEDIA`
- `NARRATIVE_OUT_OF_CONTEXT`
- `NARRATIVE_COORDINATION_SUSPECTED`
- `NARRATIVE_DISINFORMATION_ASSESSMENT`
- `NARRATIVE_COUNTER_EVIDENCE`

## 16. Human editorial gate

No automated system may:

- accuse a person or organization of disinformation;
- infer malicious intent from virality or network structure;
- label political speech as disinformation merely because it is controversial;
- equate disagreement with falsehood;
- suppress a claim because it is inconvenient;
- publish an attribution of coordinated influence without human review.

## 17. Integration

`INFORMATION FLOW → INTEGRITY → LINEAGE → CLAIM/NARRATIVE → COUNTER-EVIDENCE → VERIFICATION → CASE → EDITORIAL DECISION`

Fact-checking sources are evidence inputs. They do not automatically override primary evidence; their methods and underlying evidence must remain traceable.

## 18. Core principle

> **MALDITOESPEJO investigates information disorder without becoming part of it.**

The objective is to explain what is known, what is claimed, what is false or misleading, what remains uncertain, how information propagated and what evidence supports each conclusion.
