# NARRATIVE & DISINFORMATION INTELLIGENCE RULES

## NI-01 — Claim before narrative
Never assess a narrative before decomposing its material constituent claims.

## NI-02 — Falsity does not establish intent
A false claim may result from error, misunderstanding, outdated information or deliberate deception. Intent requires separate evidence.

## NI-03 — Virality is not corroboration
Reach, repetition and engagement do not increase evidentiary independence.

## NI-04 — Propagation is not coordination
Common propagation can arise from ordinary reporting, syndication, common source material or organic sharing.

## NI-05 — Coordination requires evidence
Synchronized behavior may trigger investigation but cannot alone establish coordinated activity.

## NI-06 — Disinformation is a high-threshold assessment
Use the disinformation classification only when both the information defect and sufficient evidence of deliberate deceptive dissemination meet the applicable editorial standard.

## NI-07 — Prefer precise classifications
When intent is unknown, use `FALSE`, `MISLEADING`, `OUT_OF_CONTEXT`, `UNVERIFIED`, `DISPUTED` or equivalent evidence-based status rather than asserting disinformation.

## NI-08 — Counter-evidence is mandatory
Investigators must actively search for credible evidence that could falsify or materially weaken the working hypothesis.

## NI-09 — Narrative drift is traceable
Changes in certainty, attribution, scope, chronology, causality or context must be recorded as transformations.

## NI-10 — Fact-checker conclusions are evidence
Fact-checking organizations may provide valuable independent analysis, but their underlying evidence and methodology must remain traceable.

## NI-11 — Political disagreement is not disinformation
Controversial, partisan or unpopular statements are not automatically false or deceptive.

## NI-12 — Network anomalies are not intent
Shared wording, timing, hashtags or infrastructure can be indicators but do not independently prove malicious coordination.

## NI-13 — Preserve competing interpretations
Where credible interpretations remain, record the dispute rather than forcing premature resolution.

## NI-14 — Harm and reach are separate
Potential harm may increase editorial priority but does not increase truth value.

## NI-15 — Historical narrative states remain auditable
Preserve how a narrative and its claims were classified at each material stage.

## NI-16 — No suppression by algorithm
Automated narrative detection may prioritize investigation but must not suppress or remove claims solely because of model output.

## NI-17 — No automatic attribution
Attribution of a narrative to an actor, organization or state requires evidence independent of stylistic or thematic similarity.

## NI-18 — No automatic publication
Narrative and disinformation assessments require human editorial review before consequential publication.

## NI-19 — Confidence is explicit
Record confidence separately from truth status, intent status, reach and editorial priority.

## NI-20 — Explain the evidence
A published assessment should allow a reader to understand the specific claim, evidence, contradiction, methodology and uncertainty behind the conclusion.
