# EARLY WARNING & PREDICTIVE INTELLIGENCE RULES

## EW-01 — Warning is not fact
An early warning represents an investigative hypothesis or risk assessment, not confirmation that an event occurred.

## EW-02 — Forecast is not observation
Predicted values and expected events must remain explicitly distinguished from observed values and confirmed events.

## EW-03 — Baseline is mandatory
Material anomaly claims require a defined baseline or must be marked uncertain.

## EW-04 — Time is part of the evidence
Preserve what was observable, detectable and knowable at the time the warning was issued.

## EW-05 — Independent lineages matter
Multiple reports derived from one source do not independently strengthen a predictive hypothesis.

## EW-06 — Alternative explanations are mandatory
Every significant warning must test credible competing explanations.

## EW-07 — Models are not witnesses
Machine-learning, statistical or other model outputs are analytical instruments and cannot substitute for evidence of an event.

## EW-08 — Probability is not certainty
A high probability estimate remains a probability estimate until the underlying event is independently established.

## EW-09 — Invalidation must be explicit
Warnings require conditions under which the hypothesis would weaken, expire or be rejected.

## EW-10 — Expiry is mandatory
Forecast windows must close. An expired warning cannot remain an active assertion indefinitely.

## EW-11 — Hindsight is prohibited
Post-event knowledge must not be used to claim that an earlier warning was more certain than the evidence available at that time.

## EW-12 — False positives are evidence
Missed and false warnings must be retained for calibration and system improvement.

## EW-13 — Alert level is not probability
`WATCH`, `ADVISORY`, `HIGH` and `CRITICAL` express editorial attention, not mathematical probability or truth.

## EW-14 — Virality cannot escalate alone
Social reach, engagement or repetition cannot by themselves elevate an early warning.

## EW-15 — Source health propagates
A warning materially dependent on a degraded, revised, withdrawn or compromised source must be reassessed.

## EW-16 — Integrity anomalies require investigation
An anomaly may justify a warning but cannot itself establish manipulation, intent or responsibility.

## EW-17 — Narrative acceleration is not event confirmation
A rapidly spreading narrative may be an early-warning indicator but does not prove the underlying event.

## EW-18 — Actor inference remains separate
Early-warning patterns must not be converted into actor attribution without independent evidence.

## EW-19 — No automatic publication
Automated early-warning outputs can prioritize human investigation but cannot publish themselves.

## EW-20 — Human editorial gate
Any warning that could materially affect public understanding, safety, reputation, markets, elections or institutional trust requires human review.
