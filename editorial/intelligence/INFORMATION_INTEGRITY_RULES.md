# INFORMATION INTEGRITY RULES

## II-01 — Integrity is multidimensional
File integrity, authenticity, provenance, context, truth and intent are separate assessments.

## II-02 — Hash is not authenticity
A hash establishes integrity of a stored object after acquisition; it does not prove that the source object itself is authentic or truthful.

## II-03 — Metadata absence is not manipulation
Missing or altered metadata is an indicator requiring investigation, never conclusive proof.

## II-04 — Original before derivative
Preserve the original object where possible and record every derivative, extraction, crop, translation or transcoding.

## II-05 — Context is evidence
A technically authentic object can still be misleading when presented with materially incorrect context.

## II-06 — Attribution must be preserved
Removing or changing attribution during propagation is a material integrity event.

## II-07 — Numerical claims require reconstruction
Verify units, periods, denominators, methodology, revisions and calculations before accepting numerical claims.

## II-08 — Temporal claims require temporal evidence
Old material presented as current must be assessed against original publication and event chronology.

## II-09 — Geographic claims require geographic evidence
Visual similarity alone does not establish location. Use a reproducible geolocation chain where location is material.

## II-10 — Synthetic detection is probabilistic
AI or synthetic-content detectors are indicators, not definitive proof of origin or manipulation.

## II-11 — Anomaly is not accusation
An integrity anomaly must not automatically become a claim of deliberate manipulation.

## II-12 — Alternative explanations matter
Before classifying manipulation, assess ordinary explanations such as compression, transcoding, editing for publication, software processing or metadata stripping.

## II-13 — Preserve disputed assessments
When credible reviewers disagree, preserve the disagreement and its evidence rather than forcing a single conclusion.

## II-14 — Corrections propagate
A material correction to an upstream object must trigger review of dependent claims and publications.

## II-15 — Historical states remain auditable
Never silently overwrite the evidence state that informed an earlier editorial decision.

## II-16 — No automatic truth inference
Integrity findings do not by themselves establish the truth or falsity of the underlying claim.

## II-17 — No automatic intent inference
Network position, editing, provenance gaps or synthetic indicators do not independently establish malicious intent.

## II-18 — Human review is mandatory
Material manipulation, provenance, context or integrity findings require human editorial assessment before publication decisions.

## II-19 — Fact-class specificity
The required integrity checks depend on whether the object supports a factual, numerical, visual, geographic, temporal, legal, scientific, electoral or other proposition.

## II-20 — Reproducibility
Material integrity conclusions should be reproducible from the preserved original, comparison method, evidence and assessment record.
